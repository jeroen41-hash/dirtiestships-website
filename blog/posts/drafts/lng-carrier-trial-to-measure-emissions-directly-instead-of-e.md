The global shipping industry currently navigates a complex regulatory landscape built largely on estimated emissions, but a new joint development project (JDP) promises to usher in an era of unprecedented accuracy. This initiative, aiming to compare real-world, direct CO2 measurements against current estimation methods, carries profound implications for EU MRV reporting, CII ratings, and the precise calculation of EU ETS carbon costs, potentially reshaping how shipping's environmental footprint is understood and regulated.

## The Limitations of Estimated Emissions

For years, regulatory frameworks like the EU MRV and IMO DCS have relied heavily on default emission factors and engine test bed data to quantify greenhouse gas output from vessels. While these methods provide a baseline, they inherently lack the granularity of real-world operational data. As Lloyd's Register's Panos Mitrou noted, the industry largely "relies on default emissions factors and test bed data to estimate environmental performance." This reliance on estimations means that reported CO2 figures often do not fully capture the nuances of varying operational profiles, specific fuel compositions, engine degradation over time, or the actual conditions a ship experiences at sea. For data-driven platforms like DirtiestShips.com, which strives for precise [emissions data](/charts.html), any move towards greater accuracy is a critical step forward.

## A Pilot for Precision: The LNG Carrier Project

Announced at Gastech 2026 in Bangkok, this pioneering JDP brings together Lloyd's Register (as independent verifier), LNG carrier owner Seapeak, and technology firms Kongsberg Maritime and Tunable. Their ambitious goal is to test whether directly measured CO2 emissions can reliably replace current estimation methods within IMO and EU reporting frameworks.

The project will outfit a newbuild LNG carrier, scheduled for delivery in early 2027, with Kongsberg's continuous emissions monitoring system, which integrates Tunable's advanced gas analysers. Throughout 2027, the CO2 readings from this system will be rigorously compared against the engine's test bed data and standard default emission factors. Seapeak, with its interests in 52 LNG carriers (including 10 newbuildings), underscores the industry's drive to "evaluate how continuous monitoring can improve emissions insight and identify further reductions across its fleet." This real-world validation could provide the empirical evidence needed to overhaul existing reporting methodologies, offering a far more accurate picture of a vessel's environmental performance.

## Broader Implications for EU MRV, CII, and ETS

The success of this project could fundamentally alter the landscape for emissions reporting and compliance. For the EU MRV system, direct measurement would mean greater accuracy in verified emissions reports, leading to more robust and credible data. This directly impacts how a vessel's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating is calculated. An accurate CII, derived from real operational data rather than estimates, provides a clearer performance metric, influencing both vessel valuations and charter rates. Companies looking to improve their [CII ratings](/cii.html) would gain immediate, actionable insights from real-time data, enabling them to optimize operations for better environmental performance.

Furthermore, the implications for the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) are significant. With shipping now part of the ETS, every tonne of reported CO2 carries a financial cost. Precise, directly measured CO2 data would eliminate the uncertainty inherent in estimations, leading to more accurate carbon cost liabilities for shipping companies. This transparency could level the playing field, ensuring that carbon costs are directly proportional to actual emissions, and influencing overall [company rankings](/companies.html) based on verifiable environmental performance.

## Beyond CO2: The Methane Challenge

While the project's primary focus, as per the announcement, is CO2, the Kongsberg system also measures methane. This is a critical detail, especially for the LNG sector. Methane slip – the release of uncombusted methane – is a significant issue for LNG-fuelled vessels, as methane is a far more potent greenhouse gas than CO2 in the short term. Seapeak's previous commitment to better methane measurement through the UN's OGMP 2.0 methane reporting framework highlights the industry's awareness and concern.

The ability to directly measure and verify methane emissions onboard could be a game-changer for the LNG sector, which is under increasing pressure to accurately quantify and mitigate methane slip. Verified onboard data for methane, similar to what this project aims to achieve for CO2, would provide invaluable insights for reducing the overall climate impact of LNG carriers.

## What This Means for Shipping Emissions

This joint development project represents a pivotal moment in the quest for accurate and transparent shipping emissions data. If direct measurement proves viable and verifiable under international regulations, it promises to usher in an era of unparalleled precision. This shift would provide a clearer, more honest assessment of shipping's environmental impact, foster more effective decarbonization strategies, ensure fairer carbon pricing under schemes like the EU ETS, and fundamentally enhance the credibility of environmental performance metrics like CII ratings. The move from estimation to direct measurement isn't just a technical upgrade; it's a foundational shift towards a truly data-driven approach to global shipping emissions.

The global shipping industry is under increasing pressure to decarbonize, with regulations like the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) and Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) ratings pushing owners to adopt cleaner fuels. The challenge, however, has always been the "green premium" – the significant additional cost associated with most alternative fuels. A recent interview with XFuel CEO Nicholas Ball reported by Ship & Bunker, however, signals a potentially game-changing development: a waste-derived marine gas oil (MGO) that claims to offer substantial emissions reductions at a price competitive with, or even below, conventional fossil fuels. If proven at scale, this could redefine the economics of sustainable shipping and rapidly accelerate the industry's path to lower emissions.

## The Promise of Waste-Derived Fuel

XFuel's innovative approach centers on producing MGO from MARPOL Annex I sludge – the oily residue generated by vessels and typically disposed of at a cost. Using their Chemical Liquid Refining (CLR) process, XFuel claims to transform this waste into an ultra-clean, drop-in fuel with impressive environmental credentials. Nicholas Ball states the product is expected to deliver GHG savings of up to 85% compared to fossil MGO. Crucially, the company aims to price its product "significantly below the price of fossil MGO," creating a compelling economic incentive for adoption. This differentiates it from most biofuels, which often carry a substantial premium, leaving owners to choose between financial outlay and compliance savings.

## Navigating the Regulatory Landscape: CII and ETS Impact

For shipping companies grappling with stringent EU regulations, XFuel's waste-derived MGO could offer significant relief. Classified under EU regulation as a Recycled Carbon Fuel (RCF) due to its fossil origin, the fuel achieves its emissions saving on a lifecycle basis by displacing crude-derived fossil fuel. This classification is vital: RCFs must demonstrate a minimum 70% GHG saving to count under the Renewable Energy Directive – a threshold XFuel expects to meet.

These certified emissions savings directly translate into tangible benefits under key regulatory frameworks. Vessels using XFuel's RCF would see a drastic improvement in their operational carbon intensity, positively impacting their [CII ratings](/cii.html). Avoiding 'D' or 'E' ratings can prevent compliance issues and potential operational restrictions. Furthermore, for vessels operating within the EU ETS, the use of such a fuel would significantly reduce their reported emissions, directly lowering the number of EU Allowances (EUAs) they need to purchase. This could lead to substantial reductions in carbon costs, providing a clear financial advantage, a factor paramount for optimizing [company emissions rankings](/companies.html).

## A New Feedstock Frontier: MARPOL Sludge

The choice of MARPOL Annex I sludge as a primary feedstock is a strategic move. Currently, ships generate this oily residue and pay waste management companies to dispose of it, or even incinerate it on board, often using more fuel in the process. XFuel estimates that 1.5% to 3% of fuel consumed ends up as sludge, translating to a global resource of 5 million to 10 million metric tonnes annually. By converting this waste into a high-value fuel, XFuel not only diverts material from less efficient disposal methods but also taps into an existing, consolidated supply chain at major ports.

The company plans its first plant in Catalonia, aiming to produce approximately 14,000 metric tonnes annually, with future scalability envisioned through replicating modular plants at key ports and exploring other waste streams. This approach creates a circular economy model, transforming a costly waste product into a revenue-generating, emissions-reducing fuel.

## Economic Viability in a Volatile Market

The economics behind XFuel's offering are particularly attractive. As Nicholas Ball explained, XFuel buys waste priced against fuel oil (given its alternative use as heat value) and converts it into a distillate-grade product (MGO). The widening spread between MGO and IFO prices—which Ship & Bunker’s G20 index shows has soared from an average of $298/mt in 2024 to $706/mt this quarter—has made XFuel's projects "probably the most profitable that they've ever been."

This fundamental economic advantage, derived from upgrading a low-value waste product into a high-value, compliant fuel, underpins the claim of competitive pricing without the typical "green premium." The fact that it's a "drop-in" ISO 8217 DMA compliant product also eliminates compatibility concerns that can arise with some alternative fuel blends.

## What this means for shipping emissions

XFuel's waste-derived MGO presents a compelling case for accelerating maritime decarbonization. By offering significant GHG reductions (up to 85%) at a price point competitive with fossil fuels, it removes one of the largest barriers to alternative fuel adoption: the cost premium. For shipowners, this translates to a tangible pathway to improve [CII ratings](/cii.html), reduce EU ETS costs, and enhance their environmental performance without crippling operational expenses. While the initial production is modest, the potential for scaling via modular plants and utilizing a globally available waste stream suggests a significant long-term impact on the industry's emissions profile as captured by [emissions data](/charts.html). This innovation highlights the critical role that economically viable, high-impact alternative fuels will play in meeting increasingly ambitious regulatory targets.

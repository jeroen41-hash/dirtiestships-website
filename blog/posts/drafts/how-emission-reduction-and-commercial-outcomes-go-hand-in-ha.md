The global shipping industry is at a pivotal juncture, where carbon emissions are no longer an environmental externality but a direct and significant factor in commercial viability. With regulations like the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and FuelEU Maritime now dictating operational and financial strategies, understanding carbon compliance has become as critical as managing fuel costs. Data from sources like EMSA MRV are foundational to these regulations, driving decisions that affect everything from voyage planning to a vessel's long-term commercial appeal, as highlighted in a recent analysis by Hellenic Shipping News.

### Navigating the Dual Challenge of FuelEU Maritime and EU ETS

The regulatory landscape, driven by initiatives from both the EU and the International Maritime Organization (IMO), is evolving rapidly. FuelEU Maritime sets greenhouse gas intensity targets for the energy used on board, pushing for the adoption of greener fuels and efficient energy consumption. Concurrently, the EU ETS places a direct carbon price on emissions, requiring shipping companies to surrender allowances for their CO2 output. This creates a complex but integrated challenge for owners, operators, and charterers, as decisions on fuel choices, operational efficiency, and even contractual arrangements now directly impact profitability and compliance. As a result, the cost of emissions can no longer be an afterthought; it is a central economic variable.

### The Evolving Market for FuelEU Compliance Surpluses

A critical aspect of FuelEU Maritime is the concept of "pooling vessels," where vessels exceeding their greenhouse gas intensity targets can generate a compliance surplus. This surplus can then be transferred to vessels in deficit, helping them avoid penalties while potentially creating a revenue stream for the higher-performing ships. The market for this compliance surplus is dynamic. According to insights shared at a Baltic Exchange webinar, BioLNG was a significant generator of compliance surplus in 2025, effectively setting the market price. However, by 2026, Bio100 emerged as the cheapest source.

The value of this surplus fluctuates considerably. BetterSea’s FuelEU index provides a daily reference price, with the 2025 volume-weighted average at approximately €193 per tonne. However, prices have since declined, reaching monthly averages of around €120 in June and €84 in October. This reduction in price reflects a changing market where purchasing surplus compliance may become a more commercially attractive option than facing direct penalties, underpinning the financial incentive for emission reductions.

### Optimising Operations: A Win-Win for Emissions and Profit

Beyond fuel choices and compliance markets, operational efficiency offers significant opportunities for both emissions reduction and commercial benefit. A common inefficiency, highlighted by Haris Zografakis, is ships sailing at high speeds only to spend extended periods waiting at anchorage. This practice wastes fuel, generates unnecessary emissions, and incurs additional costs.

The solution lies in optimising operational strategies, such as "just-in-time" arrivals, where vessel speeds are adjusted to coincide with berth availability. This approach can yield substantial fuel savings and, crucially, emissions reductions of approximately 10-18%. Such improvements directly impact a vessel's operational carbon intensity, which is a key factor in its [CII ratings](/cii.html). Better operational efficiency leads to improved CII scores, helping vessels avoid undesirable D or E ratings that can negatively affect their market attractiveness and charter opportunities. This demonstrates that decarbonisation efforts, particularly through operational adjustments, are not merely a cost but a path to enhanced commercial performance.

### Contractual Arrangements and Data Transparency

The responsibility for managing FuelEU pooling and other compliance aspects often depends on the specific contractual relationship between owners and charterers. The length of a charter period and the terms governing operational control significantly influence decisions, such as whether to sell immediate surplus or bank it for future use. For the market to function efficiently and avoid disputes, contracts must reflect realistic market values, with index-based pricing providing crucial transparency.

Accurate and transparent data, derived from sources like EMSA MRV, is paramount for navigating these complexities. ### What this means for shipping emissions

The insights from the Hellenic Shipping News article underscore a fundamental shift: decarbonisation is no longer a separate environmental agenda but an intrinsic element of commercial strategy for the global shipping industry. The intertwining of regulations like FuelEU Maritime and EU ETS with operational decisions means that every tonne of CO2 saved, every smart fuel choice, and every efficiency gain directly impacts a company's bottom line. Companies that effectively integrate emissions reduction into their core business model, leveraging data and strategic compliance, will not only gain a competitive advantage but will also be the primary drivers of meaningful reductions in global shipping emissions.

The global shipping industry is at a critical juncture, with escalating regulatory pressure from initiatives like the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) and the Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) driving a rapid shift towards decarbonization. In this evolving landscape, the recent agreement between container shipping giant CMA CGM and logistics powerhouse DSV to reduce 12,000 tons of CO2 emissions over two years through CMA CGM’s ACT+ low-carbon solution signals a growing trend of collaborative efforts to meet stringent environmental targets. This partnership, detailed by shippingtelegraph.com, exemplifies how carriers and freight forwarders are strategically leveraging innovative fuel solutions to navigate a complex regulatory environment and mitigate rising carbon costs.

## Decarbonization in Action: The CMA CGM ACT+ Solution

The core of this agreement lies in DSV’s adoption of CMA CGM’s ACT+ program, a suite of low-carbon transport solutions designed to help customers like DSV significantly reduce their supply chain emissions. According to the shippingtelegraph.com report, the ACT+ solution promises an impressive 83% reduction in CO2 emissions compared to conventional shipping fuel. This substantial cut is achieved through the use of second-generation UCOME-based biofuels, offering a tangible pathway for immediate emissions reduction. For CMA CGM, this initiative aligns directly with its ambitious commitment to achieving Net Zero Carbon by 2050, demonstrating a proactive approach to fleet decarbonization and customer engagement. DSV, in turn, furthers its own net-zero ambitions across its operations and value chain by 2050, leveraging its partnership with CMA CGM to expand access to lower-carbon shipping options for its clients.

## The EU ETS and Carbon Cost Implications

The targeted reduction of 12,000 tons of CO2 emissions over two years carries significant financial implications under the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS), which came into full effect for shipping in 2024. Under the ETS, shipping companies are required to surrender allowances (EUAs) for their reported emissions on voyages to, from, and within the EU. While 12,000 tons may seem modest in the context of the global shipping industry's total annual emissions (over 1 billion tons), every ton avoided directly translates into a reduction in ETS compliance costs.

With EUA prices fluctuating but generally high (often above €80 per ton), avoiding 12,000 tons of CO2 could save CMA CGM or its customers nearly €1 million in ETS costs over the two-year period if these emissions would otherwise have been subject to the scheme. This financial incentive reinforces the business case for investing in low-carbon fuels and solutions like ACT+. For carriers, offering such services provides a competitive edge, attracting shippers like DSV who are looking to manage their own Scope 3 emissions and potentially offset their carbon footprint. ## CII Ratings and Fleet Performance

Beyond direct carbon costs, the use of low-carbon fuels has a profound impact on a vessel's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating. The CII framework, mandated by the IMO, assigns an annual rating (A to E) based on a ship's operational carbon intensity. Vessels consistently achieving D or E ratings face increasing scrutiny and may be required to submit corrective action plans.

By utilizing second-generation UCOME-based biofuels, vessels operating under the ACT+ program will inherently improve their operational carbon intensity, contributing to better CII scores. An 83% reduction in CO2 per unit of transport work would significantly improve a vessel's reported operational efficiency. This proactive approach helps CMA CGM's fleet maintain favorable [CII ratings](/cii.html), safeguarding against potential penalties and enhancing their reputation as a responsible carrier. Accurate and transparent [EMSA MRV data](/emissions-data.html) is crucial for verifying these emissions reductions and calculating precise CII scores, providing the verifiable evidence needed for regulatory compliance and stakeholder confidence.

## Shipper-Carrier Collaboration: A Growing Trend

The partnership between CMA CGM and DSV is indicative of a broader trend of increased collaboration across the shipping value chain. As logistics companies like DSV commit to ambitious net-zero targets, they are increasingly demanding low-carbon shipping options from their carrier partners. Michael Hollstein, DSV's SVP Global Ocean Product, emphasized that "reducing emissions across global supply chains requires strong collaboration and scalable solutions."

This demand-side pressure from major shippers is accelerating the adoption of alternative fuels and decarbonization technologies by carriers. CMA CGM's ACT+ program is a response to this market need, offering a scalable solution that many leading companies are now choosing or renewing their commitment to. This symbiotic relationship not only helps individual companies meet their sustainability goals but also collectively pushes the industry towards greener practices. ## What this means for shipping emissions

The CMA CGM and DSV ACT+ agreement, targeting 12,000 tons of CO2 reduction using biofuels, underscores a pivotal shift in the shipping industry. It demonstrates how regulatory mechanisms like EU ETS and IMO CII are not just compliance burdens but also powerful drivers for innovation and collaboration. By linking emissions reductions to tangible financial savings and improved fleet performance, such agreements offer a clear path forward for decarbonization. This proactive engagement between carriers and shippers, leveraging verifiable low-carbon fuel solutions, is essential for accelerating the industry's transition towards its net-zero ambitions and will increasingly define leadership in a carbon-constrained global economy.

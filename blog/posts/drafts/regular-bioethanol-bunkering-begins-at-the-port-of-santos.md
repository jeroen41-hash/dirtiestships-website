## Bioethanol Bunkering at Santos: A New Front in Shipping's Decarbonization Battle

The global shipping industry, under increasing pressure from stringent regulations like the EU's Emissions Trading System (ETS) and the International Maritime Organization's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII), is in an urgent race to decarbonize. Against this backdrop, the recent announcement from the Port of Santos, Brazil, regarding the commencement of regular bioethanol bunkering operations, marks a significant development. This shift from pioneering trials to consistent commercial supply, spearheaded by major players like CMA CGM and Copersucar, signals a tangible step towards diversifying low-carbon fuel options and offers a crucial case study for the industry's emissions reduction efforts.

### The Bioethanol Breakthrough at Santos

The Port of Santos, in partnership with Copersucar, CMA CGM, Bunker One, and other key stakeholders, has officially initiated regular bunkering of bioethanol for vessels. This follows a successful pilot operation in July, demonstrating the scalability and safety of bioethanol as a marine fuel. The inaugural regular bunkering, on October 19, involved the 13,000-TEU container ship *CMA CGM IRON*. Notably, this vessel is equipped with the "world's first tri-fuel engine certified to operate on bioethanol," an Everllence-B&W G95ME-C10.5-LGIM, underscoring the advanced engineering required for such fuel transitions.

This initiative positions the Port of Santos and Brazil as a leading low-carbon fuel bunkering hub and establishes what partners refer to as the "world's first green corridor for marine bioethanol bunkering." With additional multiple bunkering operations for CMA CGM vessels planned by year-end, the commitment to scaling this solution is clear. The bioethanol itself is sourced from a certified sugarcane production chain, adhering to strict sustainability and zero-deforestation criteria under Brazil's RenovaBio program, addressing crucial lifecycle emissions concerns.

### Decarbonization Potential and Regulatory Impact

The introduction of marine bioethanol directly addresses the industry's need for fuels that can lower CO2 emissions and improve vessels' operational efficiency, particularly under regimes like the CII. While the exact percentage reduction in CO2 emissions for bioethanol compared to conventional fossil fuels isn't provided in the article, its classification as a "lower-carbon solution" suggests a significant positive impact on a vessel's annual efficiency ratio. For vessels tracked on our platform, a switch to such fuels would directly influence their [CII ratings](/cii.html), potentially moving them from undesirable D or E ratings to more compliant C or B grades.

The market opportunity for bioethanol is substantial. The article highlights that global demand for marine fuels exceeds 270 billion liters annually. Replacing just 10% of this consumption could represent an additional potential market of approximately 50 billion liters of bioethanol per year. Such a scale would have a profound impact on aggregate CO2 emissions from shipping, influencing overall EU MRV data and, consequently, the carbon costs for operators under the EU ETS. Reduced emissions mean fewer allowances needed, directly lowering operational expenses. This development in Santos also brings into focus the varying regulatory landscapes for alternative fuels, with other markets "still making progress on regulations for this type of bunkering," as noted by Bunker One.

### CMA CGM's Strategic Investment and Industry Trends

CMA CGM's proactive engagement in this initiative, from operating the tri-fuel vessel to CEO Giovani Grassi's emphasis on accelerating lower-carbon solutions, highlights a strategic investment by a global shipping giant. As one of the major players tracked in our [company rankings](/companies.html), CMA CGM's commitment to developing new energy ecosystems sets a precedent. Investments in such tri-fuel engine technologies and the associated bunkering infrastructure are critical for maintaining competitive edge and meeting future regulatory mandates.

The logistical complexity of bioethanol bunkering, including the development of a pioneering protocol by Bunker One to manage the fuel's low flash point, underscores the multifaceted challenges and innovations required for alternative fuel adoption. This collaboration between fuel producers, logistics providers, terminal operators, and port authorities provides a blueprint for establishing "green corridors" elsewhere, integrating supply chain readiness with ship-side capability.

### What this means for shipping emissions

The regular bioethanol bunkering at the Port of Santos represents more than just a localized fuel offering; it is a tangible demonstration of how sustainable biofuels can integrate into commercial shipping operations. For global shipping emissions, this initiative could pave the way for a viable, scalable low-carbon fuel option, particularly as more dual- or tri-fuel vessels enter service. Data from platforms like DirtiestShips.com will be critical in tracking the actual emissions reductions achieved by vessels utilizing bioethanol, providing transparent insights into their improved [CII ratings](/cii.html) and their reduced carbon footprint within the EU ETS framework. This development underscores the urgent need for similar infrastructure investments globally to support the shipping industry's ambitious decarbonization targets, moving beyond pilot projects to established green corridors.

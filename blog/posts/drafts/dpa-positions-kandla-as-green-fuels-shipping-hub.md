The global shipping industry faces intensifying pressure to decarbonize, with regulatory frameworks like the EU ETS and IMO's CII ratings compelling shipowners to embrace cleaner operations. Against this backdrop, the Deendayal Port Authority (DPA) in Kandla, India, has announced ambitious plans to position itself as a pivotal hub for green fuels, green shipping, shipbuilding, and maritime decarbonisation, a development that could significantly impact future emissions trajectories and operational costs for vessels plying crucial trade routes. This strategic pivot, detailed in a recent report by thehindu.com, highlights a growing awareness of the need for onshore infrastructure to support the transition to a low-carbon shipping future.

## Kandla's Green Ambition: A Hub for Decarbonisation

The DPA's vision centers on transforming Kandla into a convergence point for global expertise, technology, and investment, aligning with India's national commitment towards achieving Net Zero by 2070. The recently hosted "Kandla Forum 2026," themed "Anchoring Maritime Decarbonisation," brought together a diverse array of international and Indian stakeholders. This included representatives from 62 Hydrogen Council member companies – spanning electrolyser manufacturers, energy majors, and financial institutions – alongside Indian developers, shipping firms, and classification entities. Such widespread engagement underscores the industry's recognition of the critical role port infrastructure plays in enabling the decarbonisation journey.

## Fueling the Future: Hydrogen, Methanol, and Green Corridors

Kandla's strategy is not merely aspirational; it is backed by tangible infrastructure developments. The port already operates a 1 MW green hydrogen plant and is developing further green hydrogen facilities. Crucially, it has completed India's first methanol bunkering trial, signaling a commitment to diversifying the alternative fuel options available to vessels. The availability of green hydrogen and methanol, along with the necessary land, power, and water resources for their production, positions Kandla as a significant player in the nascent green fuels ecosystem.

Furthermore, the DPA Chairman Sushil Kumar Singh highlighted Kandla's role as an "anchor port in India-Europe green shipping corridor studies." This is a particularly salient point for DirtiestShips.com's analysis, as such corridors are designed to accelerate the adoption of zero-emission fuels and technologies on specific routes, directly influencing operational emissions and regulatory compliance for participating vessels.

## The Regulatory Ripple Effect: CII and EU ETS Implications

The developments at Kandla have direct implications for vessel operators navigating the evolving regulatory landscape. The availability of green fuels like methanol and hydrogen at a major port can significantly influence a ship's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating. Vessels bunkering conventional fossil fuels will continue to see their [CII ratings](/cii.html) decline, potentially falling into D or E categories, which will necessitate corrective action plans. Conversely, ships utilizing low-carbon fuels from hubs like Kandla could see improved CII scores, demonstrating their commitment to emissions reduction and potentially gaining a competitive edge.

More directly, Kandla's involvement in India-Europe green shipping corridor studies brings it into the sphere of the European Union Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). From 2024, shipping emissions from voyages between EU and non-EU ports will be partially covered, gradually increasing to 100% by 2026. For vessels operating on an India-Europe corridor, the EU ETS will impose a direct carbon cost for their emissions within the EU jurisdiction and a significant portion of their non-EU leg. The ability to bunker green fuels at a strategic port like Kandla could therefore become a crucial factor in mitigating these escalating [EU ETS carbon costs](https://www.dirtiestships.com/news/eu-ets-cost-analysis), incentivizing shipowners to opt for cleaner fuel pathways to reduce their financial burden and improve their standing in [company rankings](/companies.html).

## Broader Industry Implications and Data Visibility

The commitment of a major port like Kandla to green shipping infrastructure is a strong signal to the wider maritime industry. It suggests a growing demand for vessels capable of running on alternative fuels and encourages investment in associated technologies. As more ports follow suit, the economies of scale for green fuel production and bunkering will improve, further accelerating the transition.

From a data perspective, the uptake of green fuels will eventually be reflected in the EU MRV data collected by EMSA. DirtiestShips.com will monitor these trends, offering insights into actual emissions reductions from vessels utilizing these new infrastructures. The operational data will provide crucial evidence of the effectiveness of such initiatives and highlight which companies are leading the charge in adopting cleaner fuel strategies, visible through our [emissions data](/charts.html).

## What This Means for Shipping Emissions

Kandla's proactive stance in establishing itself as a green maritime hub represents a tangible step towards reducing global shipping emissions. By providing crucial bunkering infrastructure for alternative fuels like green hydrogen and methanol, it directly supports the decarbonisation efforts of vessels, particularly those on significant trade routes such as the planned India-Europe green shipping corridor. While the scale of green fuel production is currently modest (e.g., a 1 MW green hydrogen plant), the commitment to further development and the strategic positioning in a green corridor is a powerful indicator of future emissions reductions. The success of such initiatives will be vital in helping the industry meet stringent regulatory targets and drive meaningful change in its environmental footprint.

The global shipping industry faces an unprecedented imperative to decarbonize, driven by tightening regulations like the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) and the IMO's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII). Against this backdrop, news of major carriers making tangible shifts towards lower-emission fuels offers critical insights into the pace and direction of the industry's green transition. Japanese shipping giant NYK Line has reported that low greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions marine fuels comprised 12.2% of its total fuel consumption in financial year 2025 (FY2025), a significant step that underscores the growing viability and necessity of alternative fuel adoption to meet global emissions targets and manage escalating carbon costs.

## NYK's Fuel Transition: A Snapshot of Progress

NYK Line’s latest figures reveal a concrete move away from traditional fossil fuels. The company reported that 12.2% of its total marine fuel mix in FY2025 came from low-GHG sources. This shift is supported by an expanding dual-fuel fleet, which reached 35 vessels in FY2025, an increase from 26 in the previous financial year. These figures notably exclude LNG carriers and LNG-ready vessels, indicating a deliberate diversification of their operational capabilities beyond simply LNG-focused assets. The primary low-GHG fuel driving this transition was biofuels, followed by LNG, reflecting a strategic choice to leverage immediately available solutions for emissions reduction.

## Quantifying Emissions Reductions and Regulatory Impact

The adoption of these alternative fuels has translated into quantifiable emissions reductions for NYK Line. The company reported a total reduction of 260,000 metric tons of CO2 equivalent (CO2e) in FY2025 attributable to its progressive shift towards lower-emission marine fuels. Biofuel usage alone accounted for an estimated 200,000 mt CO2e reduction compared to energy-equivalent heavy fuel oil (HSFO), with these reductions verified by ISCC certification.

Such verified emissions reductions are not merely environmental achievements; they carry substantial regulatory and financial implications. For companies operating within the European Economic Area, lower reported CO2 emissions, as tracked through EMSA MRV data, directly reduce exposure to the [EU ETS carbon costs](/charts.html). Furthermore, improved carbon intensity directly impacts a vessel's and, by extension, a company's overall [CII ratings](/cii.html). Proactive measures like NYK's can thus enhance a company's standing in [company rankings](/companies.html) for environmental performance, positioning them more favorably in an increasingly scrutinized market.

## Biofuels at the Forefront: Current Use and Future Trials

Biofuels have emerged as a cornerstone of NYK Line's immediate decarbonization strategy. Biofuel consumption surged to 297,000 mt in FY2025, building on 251,000 mt in FY2024 and a modest baseline of 6,000 mt in FY2023. This exponential growth highlights biofuels as a readily deployable solution for "immediate GHG emissions reductions," as the company stated.

Looking ahead, NYK is also laying the groundwork for broader biofuel adoption with a 12-month B100 (100% biofuel) pilot project slated to commence aboard a car carrier in May 2026. This trial will utilize biofuel derived from used cooking oil, focusing on critical aspects such as monitoring engine durability under continuous B100 use and developing robust safe operational guidelines. The success of such trials is crucial for validating biofuels as a long-term, scalable option, addressing concerns around engine compatibility and operational reliability that often accompany new fuel introductions.

## Broader Implications for Decarbonization and Compliance

NYK's reported progress illustrates a significant internal effort to address emissions, yet it also underscores the scale of the challenge for the entire industry. While 12.2% is a notable share for alternative fuels, the vast majority of NYK's fleet still relies on conventional fuels, reflecting the global shipping industry's dependence on fossil fuels. The industry's trajectory towards net-zero will require not just incremental increases but exponential growth in alternative fuel adoption, coupled with efficiency improvements. The shift towards sustainable shipping is not just about adopting new fuels but also about ensuring their long-term supply, sustainability, and technical viability. NYK's proactive testing of B100 is a testament to the comprehensive approach needed, moving beyond mere consumption figures to tackle the deeper operational and technical hurdles of decarbonization.

## What this means for shipping emissions

NYK Line's report offers a tangible example of a major shipping company making measurable progress in reducing its carbon footprint through alternative fuel adoption. The expansion of its dual-fuel fleet and the significant increase in biofuel consumption demonstrate that existing technologies can deliver immediate emissions reductions, helping companies navigate stringent regulations like the EU ETS and improve their [CII ratings](/cii.html). However, scaling these efforts across the entire global fleet remains the ultimate challenge. While positive, NYK’s figures serve as a benchmark for what is possible today, highlighting the urgent need for widespread industry adoption, accelerated development of truly zero-emission fuels, and robust regulatory frameworks to drive the global shipping sector towards its decarbonization targets.

The latest sustainability report from AD Ports Group presents a critical paradox for the shipping and logistics industry: a significant reduction in emissions intensity alongside a substantial increase in absolute greenhouse gas emissions. This dual trend underscores the complex challenge facing global shipping as it grapples with expansion while striving for decarbonization, directly impacting key regulatory frameworks like EU MRV, CII ratings, and the burgeoning costs of the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS). For platforms like DirtiestShips.com, tracking these metrics is paramount to understanding the true environmental footprint of major players.

## Absolute vs. Intensity: The Core Conflict

AD Ports Group reported a 6% reduction in emissions intensity in 2025, bringing it down to 190.68 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO2e) per AED 1 million in revenue from 203.06 tCO2e in 2024. This improvement coincided with a robust 20% increase in Group revenue, reaching AED 20.77 billion, signaling more efficient operations per unit of economic output. However, this positive intensity trend was overshadowed by a 13% surge in total absolute greenhouse gas emissions, climbing to 3.96 million tCO2e in 2025 from 3.51 million tCO2e the previous year. This increase includes Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions, encompassing those from suppliers and customers, highlighting the growing overall carbon footprint due to continued global expansion.

## Strategic Investments in Decarbonization

The Abu Dhabi-based company attributes its intensity reduction to a suite of strategic initiatives aimed at improving operational efficiencies and adopting cleaner technologies. These include increased use of renewable electricity, the introduction of alternative marine fuels, and enhanced vessel electrification. Notable decarbonization efforts in 2025 involved the deployment of LNG-powered Pure Car and Truck Carriers (PCTCs) through its United Global Ro-Ro venture and the initiation of ship-to-ship LNG bunkering operations. Furthermore, AD Ports announced plans with Nimex Terminals to develop LNG and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) terminal hubs at Khalifa Port, signifying a commitment to future-proofing its energy infrastructure. The Group also purchased 47,960 MWh of renewable energy certificates to offset emissions from electricity, heating, and cooling.

These operational and infrastructural shifts are part of a broader, ambitious decarbonization roadmap comprising 139 initiatives through 2050, backed by projected investments of $636 million. The program targets cumulative emissions reductions of 2.9 million tCO2e, focusing on renewable energy adoption, fleet fuel transition, and low-carbon port infrastructure. As Captain Saif Al Mheiri, Abu Dhabi Maritime CEO and Group Chief Sustainability & Risk Officer, noted, embedding decarbonization into business strategy is aimed at achieving lasting emissions reductions and strengthening operational resilience.

## The Maritime & Shipping Factor: CII and ETS Implications

The report's breakdown of emissions by cluster is particularly relevant for DirtiestShips.com. The Maritime & Shipping Cluster, which generated 51% of Group revenue, was responsible for approximately 50% of total emissions. Ports contributed 24%, Logistics 21%, and Economic Cities & Free Zones 5%. The significant contribution from the maritime sector means that AD Ports' operational choices directly impact its vessel-level emissions performance.

The move towards LNG-powered vessels and improved operational efficiencies are crucial for enhancing [CII ratings](/cii.html) for individual ships under the IMO's Carbon Intensity Indicator framework. While AD Ports' corporate report isn't EU MRV data, the vessels within its Maritime & Shipping Cluster would be subject to MRV reporting if they call at EU ports. An increase in absolute emissions, even with intensity improvements, implies a larger overall carbon liability. For companies with operations extending into European waters, an expanding absolute footprint means potentially higher compliance costs under the [EU ETS carbon costs](/news-feed.html) system, which levies a price on CO2 emissions. This could directly influence a company's position in overall [company emissions rankings](/companies.html) if their global footprint translates to significant EU exposure.

## Looking Ahead: Roadmap and Responsibility

AD Ports' long-term decarbonization roadmap and the linking of senior management compensation to environmental targets demonstrate a serious commitment to sustainability. However, the immediate challenge remains the increase in absolute emissions, driven by business growth. While intensity reductions are a positive sign of efficiency gains, true decarbonization requires a decline in absolute emissions. The waste management improvements, including a 24% increase in waste diverted from disposal and a 23% rise in non-hazardous waste recycling, show broader environmental stewardship but do not directly address the core carbon challenge.

The data presented by AD Ports Group provides valuable insights for industry analysis. As detailed on [emissions data](/charts.html) on DirtiestShips.com, understanding the interplay between growth, intensity, and absolute emissions is essential for evaluating the effectiveness of global decarbonization efforts.

## What this means for shipping emissions

AD Ports Group's 2025 sustainability report is a microcosm of the global shipping industry's dilemma: how to expand operations to meet demand while aggressively reducing its environmental impact. While the 6% cut in emissions intensity signals progress in operational efficiency and cleaner fuel adoption, the 13% rise in absolute emissions underscores that growth currently outpaces decarbonization efforts. For regulatory bodies and environmental platforms, this highlights the critical need for frameworks that prioritize absolute emissions reductions, not just intensity, to genuinely steer the global shipping industry towards its net-zero targets. Companies must ultimately decouple growth from absolute emissions to make a meaningful impact on climate change.

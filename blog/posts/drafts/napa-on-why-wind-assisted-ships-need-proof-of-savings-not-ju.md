The global shipping industry faces unprecedented pressure to decarbonize, with regulatory frameworks like the EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime, and increasingly stringent CII ratings demanding measurable reductions in greenhouse gas emissions. In this environment, wind-assisted propulsion systems (WAPS) offer a promising avenue for significant fuel savings and emissions reduction, yet their broader adoption is hampered by a critical challenge: proving the actual savings. A recent article highlights that while contracts for sharing savings exist, the lack of verifiable data on WAPS contribution remains a key hurdle.

## The Untapped Potential of Wind Propulsion

Wind-assisted propulsion has moved beyond theoretical discussions, with over 100 commercial ships now equipped with WAPS, totaling more than six million deadweight tonnes, and another 100 on order. These systems can deliver substantial fuel savings, typically ranging from 5% to 20% in service. However, this wide spread in savings highlights the core issue: accurately attributing how much of that reduction comes from the sails versus favorable weather conditions or optimized routing. For an industry heavily reliant on data for compliance and financial planning, this ambiguity is a significant barrier to investment and widespread deployment.

## WAPS and the Regulatory Landscape: Data is King

The imperative for verifiable savings intensifies under current and impending regulations.
*   **CII Ratings:** A ship's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating is directly tied to its operational efficiency and CO2 emissions. Demonstrable fuel savings from WAPS directly contribute to improving a vessel's [CII ratings](/cii.html), potentially moving it from a 'D' or 'E' rating to a more favorable 'C' or 'B'.
*   **FuelEU Maritime:** This regulation explicitly rewards wind propulsion. It applies a multiplier to a vessel's GHG intensity, banded according to the available effective wind power relative to propulsion power, directly incentivizing WAPS adoption.
*   **EU ETS:** Under the European Union Emissions Trading System, every tonne of CO2 avoided through the use of WAPS translates directly into fewer EU Allowances (EUAs) a company needs to purchase and surrender. This has a tangible impact on operational costs, reducing the financial burden of [carbon costs](/charts.html) for shipowners and operators.
*   **IMO Net-Zero Framework:** While still under discussion, the IMO's future framework is expected to include fuel standards and emissions pricing, mirroring the EU's approach and further increasing the global demand for verifiable emissions reduction technologies like WAPS.

Knowing precisely how much a WAPS contributes to a ship’s emissions profile is therefore crucial for assessing a fleet’s compliance exposure and optimizing financial strategies.

## Bridging the Verification Gap with Advanced Analytics

Traditional weather routing tools were not designed to account for wind as a source of thrust. They typically treat wind as a resistance factor, making them ill-suited for ships with WAPS. The solution lies in advanced performance models that can accurately differentiate WAPS contributions from other factors.

NAPA, for instance, employs models that start from a ship’s 3D hull form and hydrodynamics, calibrated with extensive operational data. This allows them to establish a "counterfactual baseline"—an estimate of what the vessel would have consumed on the same voyage, under identical conditions, but *without* the sails. This sophisticated modeling provides the verifiable data needed for pre-installation simulations, optimized routing in service, and post-installation validation. Such precise voyage-level data is indispensable for truly understanding the impact of WAPS on overall [emissions data](/charts.html).

## Addressing the Split Incentive Challenge

One of the most significant structural obstacles to WAPS uptake is the "split incentive." Typically, the shipowner bears the installation cost, while the charterer reaps the benefits of fuel savings. BIMCO's new Energy Saving Device Retrofit Addendum, adopted in Autumn 2026, aims to provide a contractual framework for sharing these costs and savings. However, as the article emphasizes, such a contract only works if both parties can agree on what the actual savings were.

Credible, verifiable data that isolates the WAPS contribution from gains due to routing or weather is essential. This objective evidence empowers owners and charterers to negotiate fair terms for dividing savings, fostering greater investment and adoption of these critical technologies. Without this transparency, the economic argument for WAPS remains clouded, hindering broader fleet decarbonization efforts and impacting [company rankings](/companies.html) in efficiency.

## What this means for shipping emissions

The move towards verifiable, data-driven proof of WAPS performance is not merely an accounting exercise; it is fundamental to accelerating the shipping industry's decarbonization journey. Accurate measurement of fuel and emissions savings will build confidence among owners, charterers, and investors, encouraging greater investment in these technologies. While challenges remain, such as rapidly shifting wind forecasts and complex sea conditions, advanced modeling provides the necessary tools to quantify the real-world benefits. Ultimately, robust data validation will solidify WAPS as a key, measurable solution in the global effort to reduce maritime emissions, enabling the industry to meet its ambitious climate targets and navigate the evolving regulatory landscape.

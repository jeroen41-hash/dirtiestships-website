A significant step forward in curbing maritime emissions at port has been taken with the launch of the UK's first high-voltage multi-berth shore power system at Portsmouth International Port. This critical infrastructure development allows vessels to shut down their auxiliary engines while docked, directly addressing a major source of localized air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, a move that is becoming increasingly vital as global shipping faces intensified scrutiny from regulations like the EU ETS and demands for improved [CII ratings](/cii.html).

## Portsmouth Leads UK in Shore Power Adoption

The new shore power solution, delivered by ABB, positions Portsmouth International Port as a frontrunner in the UK's push for cleaner maritime operations. The system is designed to connect up to three vessels simultaneously across the port's busiest berths, offering multi-frequency and multi-voltage capabilities (11 kV 50/60 Hz and 6.6 kV 50/60 Hz). This comprehensive setup allows a diverse range of vessels, including ferries and cruise ships, to plug into the local grid. Following extensive testing with Brittany Ferries' vessels *Saint Malo* and *Guillaume de Normandie*, Virgin Voyages' *Valiant Lady* marked the first commercial cruise ship connection in August 2023. This installation is a notable achievement, particularly as the UK Chamber of Shipping acknowledges the nation's ports have historically lagged behind their European counterparts in adopting such technologies.

## Tangible Emissions Reductions and Company Impact

The environmental benefits of this project are substantial and measurable. Portsmouth International Port estimates that the shore power system will avoid approximately 20,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) emissions annually from 2027 onward. This significant reduction directly impacts the carbon footprint of companies whose vessels frequently call at Portsmouth, such as Brittany Ferries and Virgin Voyages. While individual vessel CII ratings are calculated based on annual operational efficiency across all voyages, reducing at-berth emissions contributes positively to a company's overall environmental performance and compliance landscape, improving their standing in [company rankings](/companies.html). Furthermore, this initiative is a cornerstone of Portsmouth International Port's ambitious goals to achieve net-zero status by 2035 and become the UK's first zero-emission port by 2050, aligning with broader decarbonization mandates within the maritime sector.

## Shore Power's Role in a Strict Regulatory Environment

The deployment of shore power infrastructure like that at Portsmouth is increasingly critical in a global shipping industry facing stringent environmental regulations. While the UK is no longer part of the EU ETS, the principle of reducing emissions at port is universally applicable and highly relevant to vessels that also operate in EU waters. For ships frequently calling at EU ports, the EU ETS mandates a cost on their CO2 emissions, including those generated during port stays within the EU. Reducing emissions at *any* port contributes to a shipping company's overall decarbonization strategy, potentially mitigating future ETS costs for their wider operations and improving their environmental data reported through EMSA MRV.

Looking ahead, the upcoming FuelEU Maritime regulation will further incentivize the uptake of shore power in EU ports by mandating its use for container and passenger ships from 2030, and setting specific energy targets for vessels at berth. Although Portsmouth is a UK port, its proactive stance demonstrates foresight, preparing vessels that operate internationally for a future where shore power is standard, and bolstering their ability to meet or exceed targets for improved [CII ratings](/cii.html). The port's ‘Sea Change’ project, supported by the UK Government’s Zero Emissions Vessels and Infrastructure (ZEVI) competition, underscores a growing recognition of the need for public and private sector collaboration to drive these essential infrastructure investments.

## Technical Innovation and Future Scalability

ABB's comprehensive solution, including the use of prefabricated ‘e-houses’ for main onshore power supply equipment and innovative solutions for non-standard plug-in points, demonstrates the advanced technical capabilities now available for shore power integration. The project also serves as a crucial learning experience and showcase for other UK port operators, providing "an up-close insight" into the practicalities of implementing multi-berth, multi-user shore power systems. Such technical advancements and demonstrable project success are vital for accelerating the broader adoption of shore power across the UK and beyond, contributing significantly to global maritime decarbonization efforts. This scalable model offers a blueprint for ports aiming to reduce their environmental footprint and attract vessels committed to cleaner operations. ## What This Means for Shipping Emissions

The commissioning of Portsmouth International Port’s shore power system marks a tangible and substantial reduction in emissions from berthed vessels in the UK. By eliminating 20,000 tonnes of CO2e annually, it not only improves local air quality but also contributes to the broader decarbonization goals of the maritime sector. This development underscores the growing necessity and viability of port-side infrastructure as a key lever in achieving emissions targets, influencing [CII ratings](/cii.html) indirectly through overall operational efficiency, and mitigating future carbon costs. As more ports follow suit, and as data on shore power utilization becomes increasingly integrated into emissions reporting (accessible via our [emissions data](/charts.html)), the shipping industry will move closer to a truly sustainable future.

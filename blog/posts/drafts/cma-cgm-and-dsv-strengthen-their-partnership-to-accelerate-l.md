As the global shipping industry grapples with the escalating demands of climate regulation – from EU ETS carbon costs to stringent CII ratings – major players are increasingly showcasing tangible decarbonization efforts. A recent partnership between CMA CGM and DSV highlights this trend, with DSV committing to utilize CMA CGM’s ACT+ low-carbon shipping solution to target a significant reduction of 12,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions over the next two years, leveraging second-generation UCOME-based biofuels. This move reflects a growing imperative for cargo owners and carriers alike to demonstrate measurable progress towards their net-zero ambitions, directly impacting their environmental footprint and financial liabilities under emerging regulations.

## The Biofuel Bet: A Closer Look at ACT+

The core of this agreement is CMA CGM’s ACT+ solution, which promises substantial CO2 reductions through the deployment of low-carbon fuels. Specifically, the partnership will use second-generation UCOME-based biofuels. UCOME, or Used Cooking Oil Methyl Esters, is a type of biodiesel derived from waste oils, making it a "second-generation" biofuel that avoids competition with food crops. The article states that ACT+ can reduce CO₂ emissions by 83% compared to conventional shipping fuel. While this 83% figure typically refers to a lifecycle assessment (well-to-wake) rather than direct tailpipe emissions, it underscores the potential for biofuels to drastically lower a vessel's environmental impact when properly sourced and certified. For DirtiestShips.com, verifying such claims through robust [emissions data](/charts.html) and transparent reporting is paramount, particularly as the industry moves towards broader adoption of alternative fuels.

## Quantifying the Reduction: 12,000 Tonnes, but From Where?

The stated goal of reducing 12,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions over two years is a concrete figure that provides a measurable target. However, the news article from hellenicshippingnews.com does not specify the baseline emissions against which this reduction is measured, nor the specific routes or total volume of cargo/fuel consumption involved for DSV's operations with CMA CGM. While an 83% reduction per unit of fuel is significant, understanding the *overall* impact requires context on the scale of DSV's previous conventional fuel usage with CMA CGM. Nevertheless, any verified reduction contributes positively to the global decarbonization effort. For platforms like ours tracking [company emissions rankings](/companies.html), these direct reductions contribute to a lower overall reported CO2 footprint for both the carrier (CMA CGM) and the logistics provider (DSV), moving them in the right direction on future environmental scorecards.

## Implications for CII and EU ETS Compliance

This partnership directly impacts compliance and financial exposure under new maritime environmental regulations. For the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS), which began its phased implementation in 2024, emissions from voyages within or to/from the EU must be accounted for. By using low-carbon fuels, the actual reported CO2 emissions for these specific voyages will be significantly lower, directly reducing the number of EU Allowances (EUAs) that CMA CGM would need to surrender. This translates into tangible cost savings for the carrier and potentially allows for more competitive pricing for customers like DSV seeking greener logistics.

Furthermore, the Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating, which assigns an A-E grade to individual ships based on their operational carbon intensity, stands to benefit. While the current CII calculation primarily focuses on fuel consumption efficiency and the CO2 factor of standard marine fuels, the adoption of fuels with lower direct CO2 factors (like certified biofuels) will increasingly influence a ship’s reported emissions and, by extension, its CII. Vessels consistently operating with low-carbon fuels are more likely to achieve better [CII ratings](/cii.html), making them more attractive to environmentally conscious shippers and potentially avoiding penalties or restrictions associated with lower ratings. The use of ACT+ with UCOME-based biofuels is a proactive step that could improve the operational carbon intensity of CMA CGM's fleet sections utilized by DSV. These efforts must be accurately reported via EMSA MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification) data to ensure transparency and accountability.

## Broader Industry Trend and Company Commitments

This collaboration between CMA CGM and DSV is not an isolated incident but rather indicative of a broader industry shift. The article notes that DSV is "joining an increasing number of leading companies choosing or renewing their commitment to ACT+." This trend underscores the growing pressure from cargo owners, investors, and regulators for verifiable decarbonization strategies. Both CMA CGM and DSV have ambitious net-zero targets by 2050, and such partnerships are crucial for demonstrating progress. CMA CGM's proactive development of solutions like ACT+ positions it as a leader in offering greener shipping options, while DSV's commitment reflects the increasing demand from logistics providers to decarbonize their value chains, often relying on their carrier partners to provide the necessary solutions.

## What this means for shipping emissions

The CMA CGM and DSV partnership represents a tangible step towards reducing shipping emissions, leveraging readily available biofuel technology. While the specifics of baseline emissions and total fuel volume remain undisclosed, the commitment to cut 12,000 tonnes of CO2 over two years using second-generation biofuels directly impacts both EU ETS liabilities and future CII ratings. This collaboration highlights the growing interdependency between carriers and cargo owners in achieving decarbonization goals and underscores the vital role of transparent MRV data in verifying such claims. As regulatory pressure intensifies, we expect to see more such agreements, pushing the industry closer to its net-zero ambitions through operational changes and the adoption of low-carbon fuels.

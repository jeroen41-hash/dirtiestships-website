The global shipping industry faces intensifying pressure to decarbonize, driven by ambitious targets from the IMO and the tightening grip of regulations like the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) and the Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII). Against this backdrop, news of a collaborative effort to develop wind-assisted propulsion for LNG carriers marks a significant step, signaling a proactive move by major industry players to integrate innovative, emissions-reducing technologies into a crucial segment of the global fleet. This initiative underscores the urgent need for tangible solutions that can drive down CO2 emissions and improve operational efficiency across some of the most energy-intensive vessel types.

## The Alliance: A Collaborative Push for Decarbonization

A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) was recently signed between Korean Register (KR), HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (HHI), UK-based wind propulsion technology company BAR Technologies, and the Liberian International Ship & Corporate Registry (LISCR). The agreement, reportedly signed on September 15 at Gastech 2026 in Bangkok, outlines a joint project to develop a 174K LNG carrier equipped with BAR Technologies’ WindWings® wind-assisted propulsion system.

This collaboration brings together a comprehensive set of expertise: a leading shipbuilder (HHI) for design and review, a specialized technology provider (BAR Technologies), a prominent classification society (KR) for safety and technical suitability assessment, and a major flag state (LISCR) to ensure compliance with international rules. This multidisciplinary approach is vital for the successful and safe integration of novel technologies, providing a framework for future commercialization and wider adoption across the industry.

## WindWings®: Proven Savings, New Applications

The core technology in this collaboration is BAR Technologies’ WindWings®, a system of large, rigid wing sails designed to harness wind power as supplementary propulsion. By leveraging wind, vessels can significantly reduce their reliance on conventional main engines, leading to lower fuel consumption and a direct reduction in greenhouse gas emissions.

Critically, WindWings® is not theoretical. According to BAR Technologies CEO John Cooper, the system is "already deployed across 10 bulkers and tankers, with 23 WindWings® collectively saving approximately 100 tonnes of CO₂ per day." This real-world data demonstrates the tangible emissions reduction potential of the technology. Extending this proven solution to 174K LNG carriers, which are substantial energy consumers, could yield significant per-vessel savings. Such advancements are crucial for segments that historically face higher emissions challenges, and their performance will be closely monitored through [emissions data](/charts.html) reported via EU MRV.

## Navigating Regulations: CII, ETS, and Flag State Support

The strategic application of wind-assisted propulsion directly addresses the intensifying regulatory landscape. For a vessel operating with WindWings®, the reduction in fuel consumption translates directly into lower CO2 emissions per nautical mile, significantly impacting its Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating. Vessels can potentially achieve higher CII grades (A or B), avoiding the penalties associated with lower ratings, as tracked by our [CII ratings](/cii.html) tool.

Furthermore, for voyages covered by the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS), reduced CO2 emissions mean a lower requirement for EU Allowances (EUAs), thereby directly cutting operational costs. This economic incentive, combined with environmental compliance, makes technologies like WindWings® increasingly attractive for shipowners looking to future-proof their fleets and improve their standing in [company rankings](/companies.html).

The involvement of KR and LISCR is paramount. Their role in assessing the design's safety and technical suitability against applicable rules and international requirements provides the necessary assurance for shipowners and operators. KR’s consideration of granting Approval in Principle (AIP) is a critical step towards broad market acceptance and commercial viability, demonstrating flag state and classification society support for innovative decarbonization pathways.

## The Future of LNG Carrier Design

HD Hyundai Heavy Industries' approach to integrate WindWings® on a 174K LNG carrier featuring a forward accommodation arrangement highlights an evolving trend in naval architecture. By repositioning the crew accommodation block towards the bow, HHI creates valuable deck space, optimizing the integration of large wind propulsion systems. HHI Senior Executive Vice President and CTO Hong-ryul Ryu noted their goal to "integrate wind-assisted propulsion technology into our forward accommodation LNG carrier design, further enhancing the competitiveness of next-generation, low-emission LNG carriers." This demonstrates how vessel design itself is adapting to accommodate and maximize the benefits of decarbonization technologies.

## What this means for shipping emissions

This collaborative project to equip LNG carriers with wind-assisted propulsion is a clear indicator of the shipping industry's commitment to tackling its carbon footprint. By applying proven technology to a high-emissions vessel type, the industry takes a tangible step towards meeting stringent regulatory requirements and achieving decarbonization targets. While the savings of "approximately 100 tonnes of CO₂ per day" across current deployments are significant, scaling such solutions across the global fleet, especially for newbuilds and retrofits in energy-intensive segments like LNG carriers, will be critical. This initiative offers a practical pathway to reduce fuel consumption, mitigate EU ETS costs, improve CII ratings, and ultimately contribute to a cleaner, more sustainable future for global shipping, with data from these vessels feeding into platforms like ours.

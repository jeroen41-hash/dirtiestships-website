The global shipping industry faces an unprecedented challenge: decarbonization. With its heavy reliance on fossil fuels and vessels boasting operational lifetimes stretching decades, achieving net-zero emissions demands innovative solutions to meet increasingly stringent regulations like the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) and the IMO's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII). Against this backdrop, the EU-funded MARPOWER project offers a glimpse into a potential future, developing an advanced gas turbine-based energy conversion system designed for high efficiency and crucial fuel flexibility, aiming directly at the heart of maritime transport's emissions problem.

## A New Approach to Maritime Power Generation

The MARPOWER project addresses the fundamental challenge of maritime decarbonization by re-imagining shipboard power generation. Rather than incremental improvements to existing diesel-electric systems, it focuses on developing a gas turbine system capable of operating with a range of sustainable fuels. Two years into its development, the project has completed its overall cycle and system design, with detailed designs for main components and critical technologies advancing into manufacturing and experimental testing. This holistic approach signals a significant shift, offering a pathway for future vessels to dramatically reduce their carbon footprint, directly impacting their [CII ratings](/cii.html) and a company's overall [emissions data](/charts.html).

## Fuel Flexibility: The Hydrogen Breakthrough

Perhaps one of the most significant advancements reported by the MARPOWER project is its progress in fuel flexibility. The system is explicitly designed to accommodate sustainable and net-zero fuel options, including hydrogen, ammonia, renewable methanol, and renewable methane, with minimal modifications to the combustion system. Crucially, a prototype combustion chamber has been manufactured, instrumented, and successfully tested with **100% hydrogen** in an atmospheric test rig. This breakthrough demonstrates the viability of using truly zero-emission fuels in a high-efficiency system, a critical step towards future compliance with evolving emissions standards and avoiding significant EU ETS carbon costs associated with fossil fuel consumption. For shipping companies, the ability to switch to such fuels would directly translate into lower operational expenses and a greatly improved environmental profile in [company rankings](/companies.html).

## Driving Efficiency and Reducing Emissions

Beyond fuel flexibility, MARPOWER emphasizes maximizing efficiency, which directly translates to reduced fuel consumption and lower CO2 emissions, even when burning conventional fuels. The project has made significant strides in optimizing turbomachinery, including advanced designs for low- and high-pressure compressors and turbines. A new procedure for the high-pressure turbine's internal cooling channels, for instance, has resulted in a coolant flow rate approximately **15% lower** than standard estimates, indicating substantial efficiency gains.

Furthermore, MARPOWER integrates sophisticated heat recovery technologies. A scaled recuperator prototype, successfully pressure tested at **22.3 bar**, recovers heat within the gas turbine cycle. This is complemented by a comprehensive Waste Heat Recovery System (WHRS) design, incorporating a boiler, steam turbine, and generator, all aimed at utilizing exhaust heat that would otherwise be lost. Combined, these approaches are projected to significantly increase overall efficiency and reduce fuel consumption, directly mitigating the CO2 output captured by EMSA MRV data and subsequently impacting EU ETS liabilities.

## Enabling Technologies for Next-Gen Propulsion

The development of the MARPOWER system relies on several cutting-edge enabling technologies. Active Magnetic Bearings (AMBs) are central to supporting the high-speed rotating machinery without conventional mechanical contact, allowing for active rotor control and limiting wear. A new axial AMB has been designed, manufactured, and successfully tested, alongside a multiphysics mathematical model for sophisticated control strategies. The project is also tackling the complex challenge of testing the **2.7 MW rotor system** under realistic ship movements, using similarity theory and a hydraulically actuated moving platform to simulate surge, pitch, and roll. This practical focus ensures the system's robustness and reliability in a dynamic marine environment, underpinning the transition to cleaner propulsion.

## What this means for shipping emissions

The MARPOWER project represents a significant step forward in developing genuinely transformative technologies for maritime decarbonization. By focusing on high efficiency through advanced turbomachinery and heat recovery, coupled with crucial fuel flexibility that includes 100% hydrogen combustion, it directly addresses the core challenges facing the shipping industry. As regulatory pressures from the EU ETS intensify and [CII ratings](/cii.html) demand continuous improvement, solutions like MARPOWER offer a tangible pathway for vessels to achieve deep emissions cuts, reduce operational costs, and future-proof their operations against an increasingly carbon-constrained world. The success of such projects will be pivotal in reshaping [company rankings](/companies.html) and ultimately dictating the pace of the industry's transition to net-zero.

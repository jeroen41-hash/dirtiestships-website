The global shipping industry's ambitious journey towards Net-Zero emissions faces significant hurdles, as highlighted by a recent survey of marine professionals ahead of MEPC85. While there's widespread acceptance of the IMO's 2023 Greenhouse Gas strategy, the practicalities of implementation – encompassing infrastructure, fuel availability, and value-chain coordination – are now identified as the most pressing barriers, according to IMarEST members cited by hellenicshippingnews.com. This shift in focus from "if" to "how" underscores the immense challenge the sector faces in decarbonizing a fleet responsible for a substantial portion of global CO2 output, a challenge directly impacting vessel performance, regulatory compliance, and carbon costs tracked by DirtiestShips.com.

### The Shifting Focus: From Ambition to Delivery

The IMarEST's Ship Energy and Environment Special Interest Group (SEESIG) gathered insights from a broad spectrum of maritime professionals, finding little opposition to the Net-Zero ambition itself. Instead, the consensus points to a growing concern about the *pace* and *feasibility* of transition. Alasdair Wishart, director of technical and policy at IMarEST, notes that professionals are "increasingly focussing on whether the transition can be delivered at the pace required." This sentiment reflects a critical juncture where the abstract goal must confront the concrete realities of engineering, economics, and logistics. For DirtiestShips.com, this translates directly into the trajectory of global shipping CO2 emissions as captured in our [emissions data](/charts.html). Delays in addressing these barriers mean a slower reduction in overall emissions, perpetuating the environmental impact.

### Infrastructure, Fuel, and Investment Uncertainty

One of the most frequently cited barriers is infrastructure readiness, closely linked to fuel availability. Respondents expressed confidence in various decarbonization pathways – from low-carbon fuels to energy efficiency technologies – but uncertainties around fuel production, bunkering networks, and supply chains are significantly slowing investment decisions. This directly impacts the industry's ability to transition away from high-emission conventional fuels. For example, a lack of widespread green methanol or ammonia bunkering infrastructure means even companies committed to future-proofing their fleets with dual-fuel vessels may find their actual emissions reductions bottlenecked. This inertia directly affects individual vessel [CII ratings](/cii.html), as ships are forced to continue using higher-carbon fuels, making it harder to achieve favorable A or B grades and incurring higher operational costs, especially within the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS). Vessels operating in EU waters will continue to accrue substantial carbon liabilities if viable low-carbon fuel alternatives remain scarce or prohibitively expensive.

### The Commercial Conundrum and Value-Chain Disconnect

Another significant barrier identified is the distribution of responsibility and commercial incentives across the maritime value chain. Decarbonization cannot be achieved in silos; it requires unprecedented collaboration between shipowners, operators, ports, fuel producers, charterers, cargo owners, and policymakers. The IMarEST findings highlight a "need for greater coordination" to unlock sector-wide emissions reductions. Without clear incentives and shared accountability, the financial risks and benefits of decarbonization are unevenly distributed, stifling proactive investment. This lack of coordination can manifest in suboptimal operational practices that impact overall emissions. For instance, a lack of data sharing between a charterer and a vessel operator might prevent optimal voyage planning, directly affecting fuel consumption and CO2 emissions. This not only impacts the individual ship's environmental footprint but also influences a company's standing in our [company rankings](/companies.html) based on aggregate emissions reported via EMSA MRV data.

### Operational Efficiency: An Immediate Opportunity (and its limits)

Crucially, the survey respondents identified operational measures like voyage optimization, digitalization, and just-in-time arrivals as highly effective immediate opportunities for emissions reduction. These "low-hanging fruit" measures, which often require less capital investment than fuel switches, can deliver tangible CO2 reductions today. This aligns with our observations from EMSA MRV data, where well-managed fleets often demonstrate superior efficiency. Improved voyage planning, for example, directly enhances a vessel's operational CII score, making it a critical tool for short-term compliance. However, while these operational improvements are vital, they represent only a part of the long-term decarbonization puzzle. They can optimize current fuel use but cannot fundamentally shift the industry away from fossil fuels without complementary advancements in alternative fuels and propulsion systems. The IMarEST emphasizes that while these measures exist, the sector still "requires answers on how to create the conditions needed for investment confidence for fuels and infrastructure."

### What this means for shipping emissions

The IMarEST findings paint a clear picture: the shipping industry understands the urgent need for Net-Zero, but the practicalities of *how* to get there are proving immensely complex. The identified barriers – infrastructure, fuel availability, commercial incentives, and value-chain coordination – are not abstract challenges; they translate directly into tangible impacts on CO2 emissions, regulatory compliance, and financial costs. A delay in addressing these systemic issues means continued reliance on fossil fuels, higher EU ETS carbon costs for operations in Europe, and persistent difficulties for vessels to improve their [CII ratings](/cii.html). Without concerted, collaborative action to de-risk investments in new fuels and infrastructure, the shipping industry's journey to Net-Zero will remain slow, increasing both its environmental footprint and its exposure to mounting regulatory and financial pressures detailed daily on our [news feed](/news.html).

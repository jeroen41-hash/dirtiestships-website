The maritime industry faces an unprecedented challenge: decarbonizing a global fleet with asset lifespans stretching decades into an uncertain future of fuel and regulatory pathways. Against this backdrop, RINA's unveiling of "Mermaid" — a future-ready ship propulsion concept detailed by Hellenic Shipping News — represents a significant step towards enabling immediate emissions reductions and operational flexibility. This data-driven platform, monitoring EU MRV data, CII ratings, and ETS costs, views such innovations as critical for shipowners striving to meet tightening environmental mandates and protect long-term asset value.

### Mermaid: Bridging the Decarbonisation Gap

The core dilemma for shipowners today is making investment decisions for vessels that will operate well into the 2050s, without knowing which alternative fuels will dominate, or what future regulations may demand. RINA's Mermaid concept directly addresses this by offering a "fuel agnostic" solution that provides immediate operational and commercial benefits while maintaining flexibility. As Antonios Trakakis, Energy Transition Solutions Technical Director at RINA, states, the challenge is "preparing a vessel for a future that nobody can predict with certainty." Mermaid aims to ensure today’s decisions remain commercially and technically sound for decades, regardless of how the maritime energy transition unfolds.

### How 'Mermaid' Drives Down Emissions and Fuel Costs

The innovation in Mermaid lies in its intelligent propulsion architecture, which expands the role of a vessel's highly efficient two-stroke main engine. Instead of solely providing propulsion, the main engine becomes the primary source of onboard power, reducing reliance on less efficient auxiliary engines. This is achieved through a combination of a shaft generator, a propeller shaft clutch, and battery energy storage.

According to RINA's analysis, the benefits are substantial:
*   **In-port efficiency:** When a vessel is in port, the propeller can be disconnected, allowing the main engine to continue generating electrical power directly or charge batteries. This eliminates the need for auxiliary engines to meet onboard demand, consuming less fuel.
*   **Optimised propulsion:** For vessels operating at slower speeds (slow steaming), Mermaid allows propulsion power to be better matched to real operating conditions by integrating auxiliary engines with battery energy storage in a hybrid scheme. This reduces the need for installing excess engine capacity.
*   **Fuel Savings:** RINA estimates Mermaid can reduce fuel consumption by more than 1,000 tonnes per year in some applications. Translated to CO2 emissions, this signifies a saving of over 3,150 tonnes of CO2 annually (based on ~3.15 tonnes CO2 per tonne HFO). This reduction is directly measurable and reportable via EU MRV data.
*   **Applicability:** The concept is designed for a wide range of vessel types, including tankers, bulk carriers, and container ships – segments that represent a significant portion of global shipping emissions.

### Navigating CII and EU ETS with Flexibility

The direct reduction in fuel consumption and CO2 emissions offered by Mermaid has profound implications for a vessel's [CII ratings](/cii.html) and compliance costs under the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS).

*   **CII Rating Improvement:** A vessel saving over 3,150 tonnes of CO2 annually would see a significant improvement in its CII score. For many vessels currently struggling with D or E ratings, operational efficiencies like Mermaid could be the critical factor pushing them into a C or even B rating, avoiding penalties and mandatory improvement plans. This proactive approach to operational efficiency directly impacts a company's standing in [company rankings](/companies.html) based on environmental performance.
*   **EU ETS Carbon Cost Reduction:** With EU ETS carbon prices fluctuating but generally hovering around €70-€80 per tonne of CO2, a saving of 3,150 tonnes of CO2 translates to an annual reduction in ETS compliance costs of over €252,000 per vessel. These are tangible savings that directly impact a shipowner's bottom line. The operational data demonstrating these savings would be transparently reflected in [emissions data](/charts.html) submitted under EU MRV.

### Protecting Asset Value in an Uncertain Future

Beyond immediate operational savings and regulatory compliance, Mermaid is designed to protect long-term asset value. Its "fuel agnostic" nature means shipowners are not locked into a single, potentially obsolete, fuel pathway. As future fuels and technologies evolve, a Mermaid-equipped vessel remains adaptable, reducing the need for costly modifications later. The enhanced operational resilience and reduced risk of power loss, both for propulsion and electrical systems, also contribute to a safer, more reliable asset, further solidifying its long-term commercial viability.

### What this means for shipping emissions

RINA's Mermaid concept represents a crucial step in the shipping industry's decarbonization journey. By offering immediate, measurable reductions in fuel consumption and CO2 emissions through enhanced operational efficiency, it provides a practical solution to current regulatory pressures like CII and EU ETS. More importantly, its flexible, fuel-agnostic design allows shipowners to invest in proven emissions reduction technology now, without precluding future shifts towards alternative fuels. This buys valuable time and reduces risk, making it easier for the global fleet to meet its environmental obligations and transition towards a truly sustainable future.

As regulators worldwide intensify scrutiny of maritime pollution through measures like the EU's Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) ratings and the expanding scope of the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS), the performance of vessels like GREENERGY RIVER will be critical in assessing the industry's actual progress towards decarbonization.

## Greenergy River: A New Entrant in the LNG Fleet

Yesterday, HUAJING LNG TRANSPORT PTE. LTD. (HLT), a joint venture involving prominent players like NYK; CNOOC Gas and Power Singapore Trading & Marketing Pte. Ltd., a subsidiary of the CNOOC Group; CETS Investment Management; and China Merchants Energy Shipping (CMES), celebrated the naming of the GREENERGY RIVER. As reported by marinelink.com, the vessel was built by Hudong-Zhonghua Shipbuilding in China and is the first in a series of six LNG carriers intended to transport LNG primarily to China for the CNOOC Group. The operation will commence in August, managed by OPearl LNG Ship Management Company Limited. While the article highlights the ship's role in supplying LNG, it emphasizes the vessel's propulsion system as a key environmental feature.

## Dual-Fuel Promise vs. Methane Reality

The GREENERGY RIVER is equipped with a "highly fuel-efficient, state-of-the-art dual-fuel engine that uses fuel oil and boil-off gas." The news article claims this system will "contribute to reducing greenhouse gas emissions" and "lower the ship’s environmental impact." This claim warrants closer examination from an emissions perspective. While LNG combustion typically produces less CO2 per unit of energy compared to heavy fuel oil, the critical issue of methane slip often undermines its "green" credentials. Methane, the primary component of LNG, is a potent greenhouse gas, with a Global Warming Potential (GWP) significantly higher than CO2 over a 20-year timeframe. Dual-fuel engines, particularly those operating in Otto cycle mode (lean-burn), can release uncombusted methane into the atmosphere. The marinelink.com article does not provide specifics on the engine type (e.g., lean-burn vs. high-pressure two-stroke) or projected methane slip rates, which are crucial for a complete emissions assessment. Without this data, the actual "reduction" in greenhouse gas emissions is speculative.

## CII Implications and Future Performance

For vessels operating in international waters, especially those potentially calling at EU ports, their Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) rating is becoming a defining metric of environmental performance. The GREENERGY RIVER's classification will depend heavily on its operational efficiency and actual CO2 emissions per transport work. While its "state-of-the-art" design *should* aim for a favorable CII rating (A or B), the lack of specific design efficiency data (like EEDI/EEXI values) in the news article means its projected performance remains an open question.
